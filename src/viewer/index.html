The question. For the same money, does a community of AI labs proving Lean theorems prove more when value flows back to the results a proof used (OSO) than when results are only shared (a shared library), or kept private until a target is proved (traditional)? The full design (how the targets were chosen, why this model, the setups, measures): the Lean experiment design. Results so far: AI agents on the Lean problems.

Share work early, and credit what others build on: does research get further for the same money?

Harness checks on the public benchmark

Only two settings change between societies. α, the absorption rate: the share of incoming value an idea keeps for its author (1 = nothing flows back). β: the chance an intermediate idea is published as soon as it is proved (0 = closed, 1 = open). Everything else is held fixed: 12 identical agents, equal money every round, a knowledge reward of 1 per new bit (flowing back with α) and the bounties, no validation.     Agents

    Each round, every agent picks the most promising idea it can attempt, and pays for the attempt. Its kind sets how it picks. The values are guesses, not measurements.

    What the settings mean
    How much this matters

    Under OSO rules, every team tried reaches about the top (8 of 8). Under traditional rules, the team matters: from 4.9 (all foundation builders) to 7.7 (all cheap explorers). Removing one kind changes little, except the gamer (traditional goes from 5.7 to 6.6 without it) and cheap attempts (explorers get about 3× more attempts for the same money).
